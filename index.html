Research

Pricing when shoppers ration their attention

Shoppers compare prices hardest where comparing pays. I model this as rational inattention: each product’s optimal price splits into cost, a product-specific attention markup and one shared demand term. A single formula then prices a whole menu, and calibration reduces to a linear program.

First-author paper under review at Operations Research; a related paper on learning from conversion data is at minor revision.

Spending simulation budget where the decision changes

When the best decision depends on context, most contexts are easy: one option clearly wins. CONE locates the boundaries between decisions and spends its simulation runs there, instead of spreading them evenly.

INFORMS Journal on Computing, 2026, with Y. Shen and L. H. Lee · doi:10.1287/ijoc.2025.1134

Staffing queues that do not reset

Call-centre congestion carries over from one period to the next, so period-by-period staffing misses its targets. Relocate–Certify–Repair staffs ahead of the congestion and certifies the plan with an exact finite-sample reliability bound. On fresh replications it met the abandonment target in 565 of 570 weekday periods.

Working paper with S. Shu and C.-P. Teo.

Impact

+7–8% baggage revenue, at lower average prices

Rational-inattention pricing in a randomized field test at a partner airline, 2025.

Why it works: the 20 kg tier is about 80× more price-sensitive than the 40 kg tier. The model cuts prices where shoppers compare and raises them where they buy on need. It now runs in production.

Call abandonment cut from 6% to 4%

A simulation-optimization rostering model for the NUHS call centre. Planners use it every month to set staff rosters.

Three more field projects
